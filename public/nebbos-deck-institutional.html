01 · The gap

Every department would run better with a working brain. Nothing exists that's safe enough to run inside a regulated business.

The enterprise-AI wave was designed for consumer chat and retrofitted for regulated work. It ships without attestation, without host-side isolation, and without an audit trail your inspector can walk end to end. It reads what it shouldn't, writes where it must not, and calls the vendor's servers before the compliance officer has finished reading the policy.

You already know the shape of this. The 3 a.m. incident where nobody can say what the model saw. The compliance officer manually walking Slack threads the week before an audit. The vendor that promised portability and shipped lock-in dressed as an SDK. The pilot that closed with a paragraph about "responsible use" and a footnote that says the training corpus is out of your control.

A brain that isn't safe to plug in isn't a brain. It's a liability with a status page.

03 · How it decides

Five tiers. The classifier picks the right one on your host, before the wire.

Tier 01Sealed
Journal-grade content stays on the Cradle. Nebbos never sees it, and can prove it didn't. Local weights, local memory, local answer.
Tier 02Portable
Cross-device continuity. Ciphertext-only server side. The key lives on the Cradle. Server side we hold pages we cannot read.
Tier 03Redacted-to-cloud
PII stripped on the host before egress. The provider receives the shape of the question, never the identities. Redaction rules are yours to edit.
Tier 04Attested-cloud
Enclave receipt required. The cloud call comes back signed by the hardware that ran it, and the signature lands in your audit chain.
Tier 05Air-gapped
Zero egress. Local weights, local model, local memory. Some rooms in your operation never touch the network — the Pearl in those rooms doesn't either.

The tier decision runs on your host. Not on our servers.

Classification precedes egress · always

04 · What you own

Your data. Your model. Your keys.

Data sovereignty

Row-level isolation at the substrate.

Not at the app layer. The database refuses to return a row that doesn't carry the caller's identity. A bug in the UI cannot leak — the query is refused before the answer is composed.

Model sovereignty

Every human yes/no trains your Pearl.

Not our next base model. The corrections your operators make on Tuesday show up as competence in your Pearl on Wednesday, and never leave the tenant that produced them.

Operational sovereignty

No vendor between intent and state.

The classifier that decides where a request goes runs on your host. The keys that unlock its answers sit on the Cradle in your operator's pocket. We ship you the substrate; you run the operation.

Sovereignty is a substrate, not a policy.

The three axes hold together, or none of them do

05 · How it proves itself

Every action attested. Every trail portable.

Pillar 01

Human approval, attested at the tier the action deserves.

Basic reads clear on a device-native biometric. Privileged writes require the Cradle physically present on the desk. Admin operations require an enclave-signed approval token. The approval is not a checkbox — it's a signature the substrate can verify.

Pillar 02

Hash-chained, append-only audit.

Every read, every write, every tier decision lands in a chain your inspector-general can walk end to end without asking us. Tamper-evident by construction; portability exercised by the pipeline, not promised in a paragraph.

Pillar 03

Row-level isolation, enforced by the database.

A bug in the application layer cannot cross a client boundary — the query is rejected before the answer is composed. The isolation lives in the substrate the code cannot bypass.

Pillar 04

Portability continuously exercised.

Export runs on every deploy, not on offboarding day. If the escape hatch didn't work last Thursday, we find out on Thursday — not the day you decide to leave.

The auditor's job is to verify the code, not to trust the promise.

Accountable by architecture · not by attestation letter
