01 - The gap

Enterprise AI has an attestation problem.
No one ships the substrate that closes it.

Every vendor promises governance. Almost none enforce it at the substrate. Row-level isolation is a code convention that a hotfix can weaken. Audit trail is a logging feature that a config toggle can silence. Portability is a marketing line, not a mechanism the auditor can run.

The gap is worth billions because every regulated buyer - finance, healthcare, government - cannot deploy AI at production scale without it. The EU AI Act 2027-08-02 deadline makes the gap enforceable. SOC 2 auditors discover it during the second Type II window. Enterprises have offboarded vendors whose portability was a promise, not a mechanism.

Governance sold at the surface. Never enforced at the floor.
The category is defined by the gap

03 - Why us

The moat is that governance is the substrate, not a feature.

Axis 01

Row-level isolation

Enforced at the database, not the application. An application-layer bug cannot leak a row. The row-per-customer boundary is a data-plane fact, not a middleware convention.

Axis 02

Approval-as-a-mechanism

Every consequential action gates on biometric plus hardware-attested key. No config option removes this. The approval is the write - not a signal about the write.

Axis 03

Portability exercised

Full export runs every deploy, not on offboarding day. The auditor's job is to verify the code path, not to trust the promise. Continuously proven, not annually attested.

Vs. wrapper products, run-anywhere copilots, and observability-only overlays - none can retrofit this. The three axes have to be substrate from day one. A vendor that shipped the surface first cannot rebuild the floor without a full re-platform.

Sovereignty is a substrate, not a policy.
Three axes at once - the composition is the moat

08 - Why now

The regulation just made
every alternative expensive.

Regulatory clock

EU AI Act - 2027-08-02

Annex III deadline creates a hard forcing function. Every enterprise deploying AI at production scale needs Article 11 - Annex IV technical documentation. Nebbos ships this as a substrate output, not a compliance-team scramble.

Adjacent regimes

SOC 2 CC7, ISO 27001 A.12.4, HIPAA

Same shape of requirement, different auditor. The substrate produces one artifact that satisfies each. HIPAA 45 CFR 164.312(b) audit-controls. ISO 27001 A.12.4 logging. SOC 2 CC7 change-monitoring.

Where the alternatives ship compliance-team deliverables that describe what they promise not to do, Nebbos ships an audit trail that describes what the substrate already prevents. The auditor reads a log; there is no interview.

The regulation moved the price of doing this manually up. We priced ourselves against that.
Regulatory tailwind - not a story, a deadline
