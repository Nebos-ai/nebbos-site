Every time someone on your team accepts a Pearl-proposed action, edits it,
or rejects it with a reason, that decision is a preference pair. The
proposed action, the human's response, the context they saw, the outcome
that followed — a structured record of your organization's judgment on your
organization's specific work.

Preference pairs are the highest-signal training data on earth for
enterprise AI. And in the standard vendor model, they belong to the vendor.

## Where the value goes

The standard model is straightforward. The vendor deploys a model into your
workflow. Every human interaction generates a preference pair. The pairs
flow back to the vendor's central data plane. The vendor uses them to
train the next version of their model. That next version becomes the
vendor's competitive advantage, which they sell back to you as the next
subscription tier.

Read that paragraph twice. Every dollar of enterprise AI spend is, in the
standard model, subsidizing the vendor's next moat — a moat built out of
your specific operational judgment. Your data trains their model. Their
model becomes their pricing power. Their pricing power becomes your
renewal.

This is not an accusation. It is the incentive structure. Any vendor
whose economics depend on centralizing training signal will centralize
training signal. The problem is not motives; the problem is architecture.

## The inversion

Nebbos is architected on the inversion. Every preference pair your team
generates stays in your tenant. The pairs accumulate in your tenant's
memory (Layer 07). They are queryable by you, exportable by you, and — at
any time you choose — trainable on any model you own or license.

At month one, you have a modest dataset. At month twelve, you have
tens of thousands of preference pairs. At month twenty-four, you have
hundreds of thousands of structured records that capture how YOUR
finance team resolves reconciliations, how YOUR HR partners handle
retention risks, how YOUR compliance officer files under specific state
regimes. That corpus is portable — you can hand it to your ML platform
team, to a fine-tuning provider, to an open-weight model you're running
in your own infrastructure. Your data trains your model. Not someone
else's.

## What the corpus actually contains

The interesting question is what accumulates over time. A well-formed
preference pair carries:

- The proposed action, in the exact form the Pearl generated it
- The human's decision — accept, edit, reject
- The edit, if there was one, in its exact final form
- The rejection reason, if there was one, in the human's own words
- The context the human saw when they made the decision
- The outcome that followed (where observable), and how long after
- The department, the role of the approver, the specific action class

Multiply that shape by a year of daily operation across a mid-sized
department, and you get a training corpus that has a very specific
property: it is dense in the parts of your operational judgment that
matter, and sparse in the parts that don't. That density is what makes
fine-tuning work. Preference pairs are what turn generic capability into
your team's competence.

## The twenty-three-percent benchmark

Bessemer, Avante, and SFAI Labs's 2026 AI-native benchmark study measures
what they call inference-of-revenue — the fraction of AI-native
companies' spend that goes to model inference at providers, versus
building owned capability. The healthiest tier of AI-native companies is
running at roughly twenty-three percent. OpenAI is running at fifty-six
percent. The delta is roughly the difference between spending on your own
moat and spending on someone else's.

The training substrate is what closes that gap. Every Pearl your
enterprise runs contributes preference pairs to your corpus. Every
fine-tune you run on that corpus becomes model performance you own. Every
subsequent inference against your tuned model is inference against your
moat, not against a provider's.

## The portability guarantee, in plain terms

If you leave Nebbos, you take the corpus with you. That is contractual,
non-negotiable, and exercised on every deploy so we know the routine
works before you ever ask. The corpus ships in a documented format that
common fine-tuning tooling reads directly. The tuned Pearls, if you have
run any, ship with their weights. The audit trail ships with an
independent verifier. Nothing you built inside your tenant is trapped
inside your tenant.

That is the design principle. The corpus is yours. It always was. The
platform is scaffolding for producing it, tuning against it, and running
Pearls that get sharper because of it. If a better platform comes along
in three years — the corpus moves.

## The claim

The enterprises that treat preference pairs as their training data will
compound. The ones that let their vendors keep them will spend the next
decade renting back their own operational judgment at a markup. That is
the shape of the AI-native economy, and the training substrate is the
part that decides which side of it you sit on.
