The words a company uses for its AI shape how the company thinks about
its AI. The industry default in 2026 is to call every AI-in-a-workflow
an "agent." That word is doing more work than most vendors realize, and
almost none of it is helpful to the enterprise.

## What "agent" implies

"Agent" is a word borrowed from academic AI research, where it has a
specific technical meaning — an entity that perceives its environment
and takes actions to achieve goals. In the enterprise conversation, that
technical precision is long gone. What remains is the connotation.

The connotation is autonomy. Agency. Delegation. The word invites the
buyer to imagine an AI that operates on its own, makes decisions
independently, and reports back on results. That imagination — even when
the underlying product is nothing of the kind — leaks into how the
enterprise designs around it. Approval graphs get thin. Audit trails get
skipped. Accountability gets fuzzy. The word does the damage before the
product ships.

There is also a commodity problem. "Sales agent," "support agent,"
"finance agent" — the noun stacks are indistinguishable from every
vendor's marketing deck. The word does not identify what makes a
particular AI institutional-quality. It just names the category.

## What Nebbos ships instead

Nebbos does not ship agents. Nebbos ships Pearls.

A Pearl is a per-department entity. Nebbos Finance is a Pearl. Nebbos
Operations is a Pearl. Nebbos People is a Pearl. Each Pearl is scoped to
one department, named with the department's function, and identity-
bearing — every action it takes carries its identity into the audit
trail.

A Pearl is not autonomous. Every consequential action a Pearl proposes
routes through the approval graph and requires a named human's attested
sign-off. The Pearl is the second brain the senior person in the
department would build if they had six months of no meetings and a
database degree. It is not a replacement for the senior person. It is
the connective tissue.

## Why the vocabulary matters

Three reasons.

First — the vocabulary shapes the design. When your team thinks of a
Pearl as a per-department entity, they design approval graphs around
it. They scope its reads. They audit its actions. They train the
department's specific patterns into it. When your team thinks of the
same functionality as "an agent," they design around a fuzzy autonomous
thing, and the substrate underneath gets sloppy.

Second — the vocabulary shapes the accountability. When something goes
wrong with a Pearl, the question "which Pearl and which human sign-off"
has a clean answer. When something goes wrong with "the AI," the
question dissolves into a fog.

Third — the vocabulary shapes the differentiation. "Sales agent" is a
commodity term. "Nebbos Finance running on your controller's desk" is a
specific institutional product. The words do the differentiation work
the product deserves.

## The naming pattern

Every Pearl is named `Nebbos [Domain]`. Nebbos Operations, Nebbos
Finance, Nebbos People, Nebbos Care, Nebbos Manufacturing, Nebbos FS,
Nebbos Civic, Nebbos Education. When you deploy a Pearl into a specific
department for the first time, the base Pearl is `Nebbos General
[Domain]` — meaning it is running on the shipped base tuning. Over
twenty-four months of your team's use, the Pearl accumulates your
specific patterns and evolves into `Nebbos [Domain]` — no longer
generic, no longer replaceable by a competitor's release.

The pattern is deliberate. It carries the brand (Nebbos), it carries
the specificity (the department), and it carries the maturity signal
(general → tuned). A vendor whose marketing says "our AI agent" is
carrying none of that.

## The company-brain framing

Zoom out. The idea is that every department deserves a brain that
persists past any individual tenure. The words we use for that brain
matter. If we call it an agent, we get the industry's autonomous-
delegation baggage. If we call it a Pearl — small, specific, identity-
bearing, department-scoped — we get language that matches what the
product actually is.

The company brain runs on Pearls. Every enterprise that names its AI
seriously will end up with a similar convention. Nebbos is early on it.
