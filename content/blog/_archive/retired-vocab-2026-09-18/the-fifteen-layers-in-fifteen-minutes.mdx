Nebbos is fifteen architecture layers, organized into five bands of three
layers each. Every layer is load-bearing. Removing any of them would
require the substrate to be rebuilt. This is the fifteen-minute tour.

## Band 1 · Substrate

The three layers that make everything else possible.

**Layer 01 · Data.** Postgres, per-client row-level isolation, encryption
at rest, per-client data-encryption keys wrapped by a KMS-hosted master
key. The isolation is a database primitive, not a policy. An application-
layer bug that forgets a filter fails closed — the database refuses the
read. Every client guarantee downstream depends on this.

**Layer 02 · Identity.** SSO for humans, service accounts for workflows,
workload identity for services, break-glass paths for on-call responders.
Every request carries a verifiable identity claim through the entire
stack. Row-level policies at Layer 01 read from the identity context at
Layer 02. Approval graphs at Layer 11 route by identity from Layer 02.
Without Layer 02, nothing above it means anything.

**Layer 03 · Departments.** The organizational scaffolding — departments,
teams, roles, delegation chains. First-class entities, not JSONB blobs.
Every Pearl above is scoped to a department. Every approval routes
through a delegation graph. Every audit query filters by organizational
structure. The design principle: the organization is not metadata, it is
the primary axis of everything above.

## Band 2 · Boundary

The three layers that connect Nebbos to your existing world.

**Layer 04 · Ingest.** Named connectors to your ERP, CRM, ATS, EMR, MES,
ticketing, calendar, messaging, and email. Streams land append-only in
`raw_inbound` first, then get normalized. Reconciliation between what was
sent and what landed runs continuously. The design principle: never lose
a signal because a connector misbehaved.

**Layer 05 · Contracts.** Typed contracts for every source and every
destination. Schemas versioned. Producers pinned to specific contract
versions. Consumers migrate on their own schedule. The layer that keeps
integrations from silently breaking when a vendor changes their API.

**Layer 06 · Egress.** Named egresses to your downstream systems — the
places a Pearl action actually lands in your world. Retry, idempotency,
attestation on every write. Every egress is a per-client-scoped channel;
one client's writes cannot escape into another client's downstream.

## Band 3 · Intelligence

The three layers that turn signal into thought.

**Layer 07 · Memory.** Per-client knowledge graph on Postgres + pgvector.
Preference pairs, embeddings, retrieval indices, tuned Pearl weights.
Every decision your team makes accumulates here. Every Pearl action
reads from here. The compounding-value layer — the reason your Pearl
gets sharper over twenty-four months.

**Layer 08 · Reasoning.** Multi-provider router. Anthropic, OpenAI,
Google, self-hosted, bring-your-own-model. Every call carries client
context, retrieved memory, and tool-call authorization. Provider
switching is a config change; the layer above does not care which
provider served the underlying inference.

**Layer 09 · Detectors.** Deterministic pattern detection that runs
continuously and costs nothing to watch. Deadline risk, capacity crunch,
velocity drop, handoff stall, absence signal, cascade risk. Only when a
detector fires does Layer 08 spend a model call on the reasoning. Cheap
to watch, sharp when it counts.

## Band 4 · Action

The three layers that turn thought into consequential action.

**Layer 10 · Pearl · Shell.** The per-department entity that carries the
work. A Pearl is scoped, named, and identity-bearing. Nebbos Finance,
Nebbos Operations, Nebbos People — each is a Pearl. Every action a
Pearl proposes carries the Pearl's identity, the department context,
and the specific human authorization that permits it.

**Layer 11 · Approval.** The human-in-the-loop substrate. Approval graph
per department, per action class, per risk tier. Delegation chains for
availability. Escalation on deadlock. Every consequential action passes
through here. This is the moat — the layer that makes the human
sign-off scale without removing it.

**Layer 12 · Orchestrator.** The queue that turns approved actions into
scheduled writes. Retry, backoff, idempotency. Signal fan-out to
downstream systems. The layer that makes an approved action actually
land in the world.

## Band 5 · Commerce

The three layers that make Nebbos an institution, not just software.

**Layer 13 · Onboarding.** The client lifecycle. Signup, provisioning,
configuration, offboarding. Onboarding is a first-class flow — a Pearl
deploys in three weeks, not three months. Offboarding is also a first-
class flow — the export routine runs on every deploy so we know it
works when you need it. Portability is not a promise, it is a routine.

**Layer 14 · Observability.** Metrics, traces, logs, anomaly detection.
Real-time visibility on Pearl action rates, approval latencies, model-
provider costs, client-boundary anomalies. The layer that lets you
answer "is my Pearl working?" without guessing.

**Layer 15 · Attestation.** The append-only, hash-chained audit trail.
Every read, every action, every approval lands here. The record your
regulator reads. The record your inspector-general verifies with an
independent tool. The load-bearing layer for every trust claim in every
band above.

## Why fifteen, not five

The obvious question is why fifteen layers. The obvious answer is that
this is what an operating system for the AI-native enterprise turns out
to require when you build it seriously. Each layer solves a real
problem that would otherwise break the layer above. Cutting layers means
building the same functionality later, less coherently, with more
seams — which is the pattern most enterprise AI stacks are already on.

## Why one flat commitment

One flat per-seat commitment for the substrate plus every layer plus
every Pearl your enterprise deploys — because pricing complexity is
another form of vendor lock-in. Enterprises should be able to reason
about the cost of Nebbos the same way they reason about the cost of a
core-workflow platform. Deploy one Pearl or twelve; the substrate is
the same commitment. The specific numbers are an enterprise
conversation, not a marketing surface.
